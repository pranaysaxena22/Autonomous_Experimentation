One generic robot arm does every physical step: it picks the ingredient bottles and pours them, shakes to mix, presses with a hand punch, and stirs the dissolution vessel. Nothing else in the cell moves; the balance, gauge and probe only display numbers.

Prototype, not Gazebo. This runs in the browser with a generic arm and simplified stand-in tools, to preview what the real build on a VM would show. Values and timings are illustrative.

    What the robot does, and what it does not need

    Every movement is the robot's
    • Picks the vial from the rack and holds it up to a barcode scanner, which only reads.
    • Picks each ingredient bottle from its fixed slot, pours it into the vial on the balance, and puts it back.
    • Shakes the vial to mix, pours the blend into the die, and pushes a hand punch down to form the tablet.
    • Places the tablet on the hardness gauge, drops it into the vessel, and stirs with a rod.
    • If a bottle jams, it taps and shakes it, retries, and skips the candidate if the retry fails.
    What this prototype does not show
    • Real robot reach, grip forces, collisions, or measured cycle times. The arm and its motion are generic.
    • How powders flow, mix or compress. The tools and instruments are simplified stand-ins.
    • The client's real equipment. Many labs use dosing heads and presses rather than a robot doing every step.
    • Anything about validation or regulatory readiness.

    The balance, hardness gauge, and probe are passive instruments: they display numbers but never move. The decision logic, hidden virtual lab, faults and timing windows match the dashboard demo. Everything is illustrative.
